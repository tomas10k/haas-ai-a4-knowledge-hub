# BATNA and Reservation Point

## Summary

This section covers key concepts in negotiation, including the BATNA, reservation point, and aspiration point, which are crucial for understanding negotiation strategy. It also discusses the relationship between these points, the concept of power derived from perceived BATNA strength, and the Zone of Possible Agreement (ZOPA).

## Key points

- BATNA is the Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement, representing what you will actually do if no deal is reached, and it is the key concept in negotiation.
- The reservation point, also called the resistance point or bottom line, is the worst deal you would still accept; below it, you walk away.
- The aspiration point is the ambitious outcome you aim for in a negotiation.
- The relationship between BATNA and reservation point depends on preferences; your reservation point should generally not be set lower than your BATNA's value.
- Power comes from perceived BATNA strength, and the strength of your BATNA is a major source of power and confidence.
- The Zone of Possible Agreement (ZOPA) is the bargaining range, defined as the overlap between the two parties' acceptable ranges, bounded by their resistance points.
- Higher aspirations tend to produce objectively better outcomes, even if you may feel less satisfied with the result.
