# Why Negotiate

## Summary

People often do not negotiate enough, which can stem from fears related to greed, ego, relationship damage, or a lack of self-knowledge. Negotiating is important because the payoff is large and compounds over time.

## Key points

- About one third of MBAs say they never negotiate.
- Twenty-five percent say they feel "a great deal of apprehension" about negotiating.
- Reasons people avoid negotiating include it seeming greedy, touching ego and self esteem, fearing to hurt a relationship, and not knowing their own value.
- The payoff compounds, such as negotiating a first salary can be worth about $500K by age 60 compared to a matched job type where the first salary was not negotiated.
- Negotiating at every salary change can add up to about $2M over a career.
- Bargaining ability explains 79 percent of the variation in prices for coronary stents, which range from $700 to $1,600 from the same supplier.
- Always be negotiating, as an MBA graduate negotiates every day with managers, co workers, clients, vendors and sales reps, plus in personal life.
- Everything is negotiable, as most rules can be adjusted with a well written letter and patience.
- You need to ask because others cannot guess what you want unless you tell them.
- To improve, focus on the process rather than the outcome, develop your own style, and do not copy others.
- "You don't get what you deserve in life, you get what you negotiate."
