# Salary Negotiation

## Summary

Salary negotiation involves various strategies for securing better terms, as offers presented as non negotiable are often negotiable anyway. Understanding the negotiation process, justifying requests with market data, and knowing what items are negotiable are crucial for success.

## Key points

- Survey data shows that 60 percent of offers were presented as non negotiable, with 57 percent of those negotiating and 65 percent succeeding.
- Firms negotiate for reasons including price discovery, competing for talent, benchmarking data, the cost of recruiting, tailoring the offer to make someone happier and more productive, and retention.
- Salary compounds because inflation adjustments, performance bonuses, and raises are calculated as a percentage of the base salary, leading to significant differences over time based on negotiation.
- Negotiable items include salary, signing bonus, start date, equity (understanding RSUs versus stock options, strike price, vesting schedule, and time window to exercise), job title, location, team, remote work, relocation stipend, performance bonus, time off, and visa support.
- The job negotiation process involves preparing, receiving the offer, counteroffering, and then accepting or rejecting the offer, followed by celebration.
- The strongest argument for a counteroffer is the market rate, which is the most competitive argument in the fight for talent, and comparables must be comparable by industry and/or role.
- To avoid salary history bans, ask people who may be at the top of the earnings range, even if they do not share your background.
