# Team Negotiations

## Summary

Team negotiations involve benefits such as increased expertise and ideas, roles that manage complexity, greater acceptance of the agreement, and more perceived power. However, teams also incur costs like coordination effort, internal competition, and reduced trust.

## Key points

- Benefits of a team include more expertise and ideas.
- Teams provide roles that handle complexity.
- Teams result in greater acceptance of the agreement and more perceived power.
- Costs associated with teams include coordination effort, internal competition, and less trust.
- Before using a team, consider the time available, the complexity of the deal, and the strength of team boundaries.
- Organizing a team involves planning roles in advance, presenting a united front, using caucuses (private team breaks), and agreeing on signals.
- Suggested roles include a lead authorized to make or reject offers, a secondary negotiator who keeps the lead on track or takes over, and a process or strategy person who talks less and analyzes more.
