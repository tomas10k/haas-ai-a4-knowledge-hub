# Weak BATNA and Irrational Negotiators

## Summary

This section discusses strategies for dealing with weak BATNAs and how to handle negotiators who may appear irrational. It provides advice on improving negotiation positions, sharing information strategically, and using perspective-taking to understand the counterpart's interests.

## Key points

- Strategies for handling a weak BATNA include improving it, creating value first, focusing on the relationship, redirecting attention to the counterpart's BATNA, or not negotiating.
- In the Teddy Roosevelt story, the campaign manager asked how much a studio would pay for using a photograph, and the studio replied it would be pleased to pay $250.
- A seller should let the buyer craft a more creative, value expanding offer, such as including employment, when openly sharing real interests, such as a one-year retreat and a need for a job on return.
- Labeling someone irrational limits one's own options, as noted by Malhotra and Bazerman (2007).
- To address different information or perspectives, ask, "Why do you care about this? Here is the information as I see it; how do you see it?"
- Use perspective taking (what would I do in their situation?) and perspective getting (asking them for their view).
