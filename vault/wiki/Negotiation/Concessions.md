# Concessions

## Summary

Concessions involve specific rules for negotiation to ensure fairness and preserve relationships. Good concessions are principled, reciprocated, and small, while avoiding extremes like total non-negotiation or excessive fighting over small amounts.

## Key points

- Do not set your reservation point too close to your aspiration point; leave room to concede.
- Never make unilateral concessions; ask for reciprocity.
- Label your concessions so they are recognized.
- Do not say "this is my first and final offer."
- Use silence and patience.
- Treat sharing information as a concession too.
- Do not negotiate with yourself.
- Good concessions are principled (with a clear rationale), reciprocated (earned through effort, which makes them more appreciated), and small, shrinking as you approach your reservation point.
- Avoid two extremes: not negotiating at all out of fear of hurting the relationship, and fighting over every penny until the relationship is damaged.
- Agreeing with resentment can hurt the relationship too.
- Negotiations are almost always a repeated game, and standing your ground does not threaten the relationship if done right.
