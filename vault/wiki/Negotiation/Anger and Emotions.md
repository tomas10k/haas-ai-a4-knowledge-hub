# Anger and Emotions

## Summary

This section discusses the role of anger and emotions in negotiation, using the Myti-Pet case as an example. It details the costs associated with anger in negotiations, potential benefits, methods for de-escalation, and strategies for addressing power dynamics to achieve mutually agreeable solutions.

## Key points

- Anger escalates conflict, biases perception, increases impasses, reduces joint gains, reduces understanding of the counterpart's interests, increases offer rejection and retaliation, and can cost more for women and minorities.
- Possible benefits of anger include extracting concessions, feeling more powerful, and motivating action; suppressing anger can also be maladaptive.
- To de-escalate, strategies include taking breaks, changing the lead negotiator, removing an overly emotional team member, showing you could match the anger but choosing not to escalate, and pointing out that both sides can keep blaming each other or move toward a mutually agreeable solution.
- Myti-Pet's growth and wheat flour business gave it leverage against Rawmat, who looked powerful as the large supplier.
- Negotiating the future is often a good way to resolve past issues, and a good solution included Rawmat reimbursing up to $200K of retooling, Myti-Pet paying at least part of the $250K for the last shipment, volume pricing for meat flour, a three-year contract covering both flour types, tighter specifications with penalties, and a new delivery schedule.
